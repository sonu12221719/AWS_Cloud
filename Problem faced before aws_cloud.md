Before AWS was created, Amazon faced several critical challenges related to its infrastructure and scalability. Here are the key problems they encountered:  

### **1. Scalability Issues**  
- **Amazon's E-commerce Growth:** As Amazon's online retail business grew rapidly, it needed to scale its infrastructure efficiently.  
- **Traditional Data Centers:** Amazon initially relied on traditional on-premises servers, which were difficult to scale quickly.  
- **High Infrastructure Costs:** Setting up new servers was expensive and time-consuming.  

### **2. Inefficient Resource Utilization**  
- Amazon had **large, underutilized server farms** that were only fully used during peak shopping seasons (like Black Friday).  
- The remaining time, these servers sat idle, leading to **wasted resources and costs**.  

### **3. Slow Development and Deployment**  
- Developers at Amazon had to **rebuild infrastructure** for every new service or application, leading to delays.  
- Setting up databases, compute resources, and networking took weeks or months.  
- This slowed down innovation and new feature releases.  

### **4. Complexity in Managing Infrastructure**  
- Amazon had **separate IT teams managing different parts of the infrastructure** (servers, databases, networking).  
- Developers had to work closely with system administrators, making deployments inefficient.  
- Managing and maintaining hardware became **too complex and time-consuming**.  

### **5. The Need for a Better Architecture**  
- Amazon realized they needed a **standardized, scalable, and automated** infrastructure.  
- They decided to **turn their internal cloud infrastructure into a service** that other businesses could use.  

---

### **How AWS Solved These Problems**  
- **Elastic Computing (EC2)**: Businesses could now scale servers up or down as needed.  
- **Pay-as-you-go Model**: Customers only paid for the resources they used, reducing wasted costs.  
- **Automated Deployments**: AWS services like S3, Lambda, and RDS automated tasks, making it easier to deploy applications.  
- **Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)**: Developers could provision resources instantly, eliminating hardware delays.  

Thus, Amazon **launched AWS in 2006**, turning their solution into a **global cloud computing platform** that revolutionized the industry. 🚀
